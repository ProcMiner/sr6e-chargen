SR6e CharGen

User Guide

How to build a character, finish it up, and take it from the Character Vault into an actual game — plus how the GM's Bar works if you're running the table.

Curious what's implemented from the rulebook vs. not yet? See Rules Coverage. Curious what's shipped recently? See Release Notes.

Contents

    Overview

    The app has three areas, all reachable from the tab bar at the top of every page:

    • Character Vault — where you create characters and build them out (attributes, skills, gear, spells, everything). This is chargen mode.
    • In Play — pick a finished character to actually play with: track damage, Edge, Karma/Nuyen earned in session, Reputation/Heat, and everything else that changes at the table.
    • GM's Bar — if you're running the game: create a session with a join code, watch your players' vitals from one screen, and keep an NPC roster.

    A character always starts in the Character Vault, gets built there, and only shows up in In Play / GM's Bar once it exists — there's no separate "publish" step beyond just saving it.

    Starting a Character

    From Character Vault, type a name into the box at the top, pick a build system from the dropdown next to it, and click New Character. That drops you straight into the builder.

    You get two build systems to choose from, and they produce the same kind of character — they just get you there differently:

    • Priority System — the classic method. You rank five categories (Metatype, Attributes, Skills, Magic or Resonance, Resources) from A to E, and each ranking hands you a bigger or smaller pool to spend in that category.
    • Life Path (Modules) — build a backstory instead: pick life modules (Ganger, Corporate, Military, College, and dozens more) in order, and your attributes/skills/gear/nuyen come out of the modules you picked rather than a points table.

    Neither is "better" — it's a table preference. Priority is faster if you know exactly what character you want; Life Path is more fun if you want the sheet to fall out of a story. Both builders are step-by-step wizards — a step rail across the top, one focused screen at a time. They differ only in their front end (how you determine your character's core build); both converge on the exact same closing steps — Qualities, Customization Karma, Buy Gear, Finishing Steps, Final Calculations (see The Shared Ending).

    You can't switch systems on an existing character. The choice is locked in at creation (it's baked into how the build itself is validated). If you want to try the other system, start a new character.

    Building with the Priority System

    The builder walks you through eleven steps, one screen at a time, in the same order the core rulebook itself uses (p.63-67). A step rail across the top lists all eleven — click any step's name to jump straight to it. Nothing is locked: the rulebook explicitly says priority selections don't have to be made in a fixed order, so feel free to jump back and adjust an earlier choice after seeing a later one. A summary sidebar stays visible on every step, showing your attributes, derived stats (Initiative, Attack Rating, Defense Rating), qualities, and remaining Karma/Nuyen/Adjustment Points no matter which step you're on.

    1. Priorities

    A Power Level toggle at the top: Standard, Street-level, or Prime Runner. Street-level makes every priority row read one letter worse (a tougher, grittier start); Prime Runner doubles your customization Karma from 50 to 100 (a stronger, more experienced start). Leave it on Standard unless your GM says otherwise. Below that, five dropdowns — Metatype, Attributes, Skills, Magic or Resonance, Resources — each gets a different letter A through E, no repeats. Put your highest priority on whatever matters most to your concept: A in Resources for a gear-heavy rigger, A in Magic for a dedicated spellcaster, and so on.

    2. Metatype

    Buttons appear for the metatypes your Metatype letter unlocks (Human is only available at low priorities; Dwarf/Elf/Ork/Troll need progressively higher ones). Pick one. If a Metavariant row appears below it, that's an optional reskin of your base metatype (from the Sixth World Companion) with its own attribute ranges — it costs Karma from your customization pool, so it's entirely optional. Below that, an Edge stepper: Edge is funded from your Metatype's Adjustment Points pool, not the Attributes pool in the next step — the sidebar tracks how much of that pool you have left as you spend it here and across the next two steps.

    3. Attributes

    A point pool (its size depends on your Attributes priority letter) to spend across Body, Agility, Reaction, Strength, Willpower, Logic, Intuition, and Charisma using the +/- buttons. Pushing an attribute above 6 is a "special racial attribute" increase and normally has to be paid for from Adjustment Points instead of this pool once you're past 6 — there's a per-attribute "Fund From Adjustment Points" checkbox (a house rule this table uses) that lets you pay for the attribute's entire value from Adjustment Points instead, freeing up regular points for everything else.

    4. Skills

    Same idea as Attributes — a point pool, spent with +/- buttons across the skill list. Right below it, Specializations lets you pick one narrow focus per skill (free at creation, one per skill) for +2 dice on tests in that specific area.

    5. Magic/Resonance

    If you want a magical or technomancer character, pick one here: Full magician, Aspected magician, Mystic Adept, Adept, or Technomancer. Skip this entirely for a mundane character — nothing else on this step will show up, and that's expected. Picking a magic option unlocks a Tradition Attribute choice (Logic or Charisma) and a boost stepper that spends the rest of your Adjustment Points raising Magic or Resonance above its base rating, up to 6. Technomancers also get a Living Persona panel here to split their bonus Matrix attribute points.

    6. Resources

    Shows your starting nuyen based on your Resources priority letter. Click Apply to add it to your pool — you'll spend it later in Lifestyle, PACKs, and Gear.

    7. Select Qualities

    Pick up to 6 Qualities (positive and negative) from the catalog — your net Karma from Qualities is capped at +20. Racial qualities from your metatype don't count against the 6-quality cap.

    8. Customization Karma

    Everyone gets a customization Karma pool (50, or 100 with Prime Runner). This step spends it on Spells, Adept Powers, and/or Complex Forms, whichever apply to your Magic/Resonance choice — every one of these is skipped automatically for a mundane character. Magicians and Technomancers get a free allotment before Karma costs kick in; whatever Customization Karma you don't spend here carries forward into Buy Gear below, where you can convert it to nuyen or spend it bonding foci. If you're Awakened or a Technomancer, there's also an optional Initiation / Submersion section for starting with an Initiate/Submersion Grade already earned (an "experienced character" house rule, not a printed core-rulebook option) — pick a Metamagic or Echo from the catalog, or skip it entirely to start at Grade 0. A Skills & Specializations section lets you spend leftover Karma raising a skill rank or buying a new specialization (core p.66's own list of what Customization Karma covers) — skills cap at 6 here (7 if you have the Aptitude quality), and Expertise isn't purchasable until career mode, both straight from the book.

    9. Buy Gear

    Pick a Lifestyle tier and prepay months, browse PACKs (pre-assembled gear bundles, including full Complete Character PACKs), and shop the full Gear catalog — all from the same nuyen pool. Anything with Essence cost or Karma-bonding cost (cyberware, bioware, foci) is tracked automatically against your Essence and Karma pools as you buy it. A Custom Cyberdeck builder sits between Gear and Decker Persona if you'd rather assemble your own deck than buy one of the six stock models — choose a Core Rating plus Attack/Sleaze Module ratings within a Core Slot budget, and it's added as a priced gear line like anything else (pair it with one of the new Cyberhack catalog entries for Data Processing/Firewall). A Karma slider lets you cover part or all of the build cost in Karma instead of nuyen (4,000¥ per Karma, the book's "Building Your Own" DIY option) if you'd rather spend Karma than cash. If you buy a cyberdeck (stock or custom) or a commlink/cyberjack pair, a Decker Persona panel appears here too, letting you assign the printed Attack/Sleaze/Data Processing/Firewall numbers from your devices across the four Matrix attribute slots — except a custom deck's Attack/Sleaze, which are locked to it and always show up fixed.

    10. Finishing Steps

    Contacts: click Add, then spend Charisma × 6 points raising Connection and Loyalty on your contacts (no single rating can exceed your Charisma). Knowledge & Language Skills: every character gets one free Native language; beyond that, spend a Logic-sized budget on knowledge topics or additional language levels (Basic, then Specialist, then Expert).

    11. Final Calculations

    A full-width copy of the summary sidebar, for one last read-through: attributes, derived stats, qualities, gear, and your final Karma/Nuyen/Adjustment Points totals, all in one place. Click Save in the header any time — nothing saves automatically while you're building, so save before you navigate away.

    Building with Life Path (Modules)

    Life Path walks you through your character's life in order — where the Priority System asks "how good are you," Life Path asks "what did you do." Like Priority, it's a step-by-step wizard with a clickable step rail across the top you can jump around on freely; unlike Priority's five priority categories, its four front-end steps read best top to bottom, since each one narratively builds on the last (the Sixth World Companion's own text: "the order you select modules represents how your life progressed").

    1. Born This Way

    Pick your metatype: Human, Dwarf, Elf, Ork, or Troll. No priority letters involved here — Life Path metatypes just have their own fixed attribute ranges. Then choose Mundane, Awakened (Full Magician/Aspected Magician/Mystic Adept/Adept), or Emerged (Technomancer) — this grants your starting Magic or Resonance and unlocks a Tradition Attribute choice for magicians and a Living Persona panel for technomancers, right here on this step.

    2. Growing Up: Early Childhood and Adolescence

    Choose 4 skills from the list shown — each comes in at rank 2. This represents what you picked up before adulthood.

    3. Coming of Age

    Choose your strongest skill (rank 4, or rank 6 if you also picked it in Growing Up) and your best attribute (gains +5 — everything except Edge, Magic, and Resonance is eligible). This step also grants 1-2 qualities (pick them in the Select Qualities step), +25,000¥ (added once you make a skill choice), and 4 contact points to spend in Finishing Steps.

    4. Adult Life Modules

    The heart of Life Path: pick up to 8 modules from a long list (Academy Training, Corporate Intern, Ganger, Military, Street Doc, dozens more) via Add a module, grouped into Choices (careers, training, time spent) and Events (one-time occurrences like Harsh Truth or Windfall) — both are pulled from the same 8 slots, so mix and match as you see fit. Each module grants its own mix of skills, attribute bumps, nuyen, contact points, and sometimes gear or qualities — read a module's summary before adding it, since they vary a lot in what they grant. Awakened-only and Emerged-only modules are labeled as such and only make sense if you picked a matching path. This is the one step you'll likely revisit repeatedly rather than fill in once, since you keep coming back to it until all your slots are used.

    A Standard / Elite toggle sits at the top of this step (Companion p.16): Elite bumps you to 10 module slots instead of 8, for a higher-level corporate operative, celebrity, or executive concept. Leave it on Standard unless your GM says otherwise.

    Once you're happy with your module picks, continue into the same closing steps Priority uses — see The Shared Ending below.

    Life Path's Contacts and Knowledge/Language budgets come entirely from the modules you picked (plus Coming of Age) rather than from Charisma or Logic the way the Priority System's do — that's a genuine rules difference between the two systems, not a bug. Life Path contacts can also be pushed further with customization Karma if you want to exceed what your modules granted.

    The Shared Ending: Qualities Through Final Calculations

    Both builders land on these exact same five wizard steps once their own front end is done — steps 7-11 for Priority, steps 5-9 for Life Path. The Sixth World Companion (p.30) explicitly funnels Life Path into the same Core Rulebook steps Priority uses, so this section describes both at once; the one place they genuinely differ is Finishing Steps, called out below.

    Select Qualities

    Pick up to 6 Qualities (positive and negative) from the catalog — your net Karma from Qualities is capped at +20. Racial qualities from your metatype don't count against the 6-quality cap.

    Customization Karma

    If you picked a magic or Resonance path, this step has Spells, Adept Powers, and/or Complex Forms to spend your customization Karma on, plus an optional Initiation / Submersion section for starting with a Grade already earned — every one of these is skipped automatically for a mundane character. A Skills & Specializations section is always available regardless of magic/Resonance, letting you spend leftover Karma raising a skill rank or buying a new specialization (skills cap at 6 here, 7 with Aptitude; Expertise waits for career mode).

    Buy Gear

    Pick a Lifestyle tier and how many months you're prepaying, browse PACKs (pre-assembled gear bundles at a flat price, including full Complete Character PACKs — some bundle a Shadowrunner Starter PACK and a month of lifestyle automatically), and shop the full item-by-item Gear catalog — all from the same nuyen pool, so it's worth deciding your lifestyle before a shopping spree. Anything with Essence cost or Karma-bonding cost (cyberware, bioware, foci) is tracked automatically against your Essence and Karma pools as you buy it. A Custom Cyberdeck builder sits between Gear and Decker Persona if you'd rather assemble your own deck than buy one of the six stock models — choose a Core Rating plus Attack/Sleaze Module ratings within a Core Slot budget, and it's added as a priced gear line like anything else (pair it with one of the new Cyberhack catalog entries for Data Processing/Firewall). A Karma slider lets you cover part or all of the build cost in Karma instead of nuyen (4,000¥ per Karma, the book's "Building Your Own" DIY option) if you'd rather spend Karma than cash. If you own a cyberdeck (stock or custom) or a commlink/cyberjack pair, a Decker Persona panel appears here too, letting you assign the printed Attack/Sleaze/Data Processing/Firewall numbers from your devices across the four Matrix attribute slots — except a custom deck's Attack/Sleaze, which are locked to it and always show up fixed.

    Finishing Steps

    Contacts: click Add, then spend points raising Connection and Loyalty ratings. How many points you have and where they come from depends on your build system — Charisma × 6 for Priority, module-granted (plus Coming of Age) for Life Path, which can also push a rating further using customization Karma. Knowledge & Language Skills (Priority only — Life Path's knowledge/language comes entirely from module choices instead, see above): every character gets one free Native language; beyond that, spend a Logic-sized budget on knowledge topics or additional language levels (Basic, then Specialist, then Expert).

    Final Calculations

    A full-width copy of the right-hand summary sidebar (which tallies your character live on every step, no matter which builder you're using), for one last read-through: attributes, derived stats (Condition Monitors, Initiative, Attack/Defense Rating), skills, qualities, gear, and your final Karma/Nuyen totals. Click Save in the header any time — nothing is saved automatically while you're building, so save before you navigate away. Download Character Sheet fills in the official Genesis character sheet PDF with your current build.

    Moving a Character into Play

    There's no explicit "finish" button — a character is playable as soon as it's saved. When you're ready to actually run it at the table:

    1. Go to In Play in the top bar. This lists every character you own.
    2. Click the character you want to play. This opens its Live Play page.
    3. If your GM has already created a session, enter the join code they gave you under Games and click Join a game. You can be in more than one game with the same character, and leave a game later with Leave.

    From here the page is your character's dashboard for actually playing: Physical/Stun damage trackers, Edge, a Combat reference panel (Attack Rating, Defense Rating, and reference tables), Reputation & Heat, Karma & Advancement (award Karma/Nuyen from a run, spend Karma raising attributes/skills, track Initiation/Submersion), and — if applicable — Spirits, The Astral Plane, and The Matrix panels. Everything here saves automatically a moment after you change it; there's no separate save step once you're in Live Play.

    You can jump back into the builder (Character Vault) at any time to keep shopping or adjusting your build — Live Play and the builder both read and write the same character, so changes show up in both places.

    GM's Bar

    Everything here is for running the table, not playing a single character.

    Creating and running a session

    1. Go to GM's Bar in the top bar and click Create [your username]'s Game the first time (you only need one session per campaign — players join it once and stay).
    2. Share the join code shown at the top of your session with your players — they enter it on their own character's Live Play page.
    3. Click into your session from the list to see every connected character at a glance: Physical/Stun damage bars, Edge, Reputation/Heat (plus the party's highest Heat, since that's what actually matters for group effects), and any active status effects.

    This view polls automatically every few seconds, so you don't need to refresh the page during a session — just keep it open on a second screen.

    NPC roster

    A separate GM-only library, further down the same page, reusable across every game you run (it isn't tied to one session). Click Add NPC for a blank stat block, or use Import from book to pull in one of the pre-built Grunt, Prime Runner, and Critter stat blocks straight from the rulebooks — searchable by name, and grouped by sourcebook. Each imported NPC gets its own live damage tracker, same as a player character's, and you can minimize/expand each card to keep the list manageable once it grows.

    Full Build for an NPC

    The roster's normal stat block is deliberately quick and light — a description, flat Physical/Stun numbers, and free-text notes on combat and tactics. That's fine for a mook or a one-scene guard, but sometimes an NPC deserves better: a named rival, a fixer who recurs all campaign, or the BBEG the whole run is building toward.

    Every NPC card has a Build Full Character button next to Expand and Delete. Click it and you land in the exact same character builder a player uses — choose Priority System or Life Path, then work through attributes, skills, qualities, spells or adept powers, gear, everything, exactly as described in Building with the Priority System / Building with Life Path above. The Summary sidebar computes the same real numbers a player character gets: Condition Monitors, Initiative, Attack Rating, Defense Rating, and so on. Save and Download Character Sheet both work here too, so you can hand your BBEG a real printed sheet if you want one.

    Once an NPC has a full build, its roster card's button changes to Edit Full Build so you can jump back in and adjust it later — leveling up a recurring villain between sessions, for example.

    The full build and the simple stat block are independent. Building out a full character does not change the Physical/Stun damage tracker on the roster card — that still uses the simple numbers you set (or the ones an imported template came with). If you want the tracker to match your full build's real Condition Monitor, copy those numbers over by hand in the NPC's Details section. Live damage tracking driven directly off a full build isn't built yet.
    Written by walking through both build systems and the In Play / GM's Bar flows directly in the app, not from memory — matching the real button labels and section order as of when this was written. If a step here stops matching what you actually see, that's this page falling behind a UI change, not the app being wrong.
